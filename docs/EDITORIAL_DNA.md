# Editorial DNA — the reference customer, *Albert Deep Dive*

> *Albert Deep Dive* is the newsletter of Briefly's first customer, Albert School. Its first issue
> is the reference Briefly's default sections, formats and print design were derived from; nothing
> below describes Briefly itself.

This document is the distilled analysis of the reference issue supplied with the brief
(*Albert's Deep Dive — Special issue N°1, May 2025*, 89 pages in the DOCX, tabloid
26 × 37 cm, editor-in-chief Milan Viallet, translator Khadidja Addi). It separates what
makes the publication *Albert Deep Dive* (to preserve) from execution details that were
accidents of a manual Word workflow (to improve).

## 1. What the publication is

A monthly, student-run, English-language newspaper of Albert School (a French
business + data school with campuses in Paris, Lyon, Marseille and — at the time of the
issue — an opening in Geneva; partnered with Mines Paris PSL and the Eugenia sister
school). It is written by students for students, staff and the school's ecosystem. It is
proud, warm, slightly cheeky, and unusually technical for a student paper: articles quote
LightGBM rankers, LSTMs, Gaussian mixtures, Folium/Streamlit/Power BI without
apologising.

Two ideas define it:

1. **Business Deep Dives (BDD)** — the school's signature three-week company case
   projects — are the editorial spine. The issue devotes roughly a third of its pages to them.
2. **"Albert energy"** — school pride, campus rivalry (Marseille tops the maths tests),
   student initiative (a digital agency, an association, a regatta crew), and inside jokes.

## 3. Article formats (the grammar of the paper)

- **Interview (Q&A)** — capitalised question headings ("CAN YOU INTRODUCE YOURSELF?"),
  first-person answers, one big pull quote, a portrait photo, an "In a nutshell" bullet box.
- **Profile** — third-person feature on one person, 3–4 capitalised crossheads, a quote as
  headline ("I'm the Hugo Décrypte of Playmobil!"), photo + caption.
- **BDD case** — headline = *Company – Cohort Campus* ("Carrefour – B2 Paris"). Body
  covers: the company in one paragraph, the dates, the mission/problem, the data, the
  methods (named tools/models), the winning team (named), sometimes finalists, jury
  members, and a first-person testimony from the winners. Visual assets: company logo,
  winning team photo (often with jury), dashboard screenshots, methodology diagrams,
  pitch photo. Byline "Article : NAME".
- **News brief** — 3–8 paragraphs, one crosshead, one photo with caption and © credit.
- **Digest** — several short items with capitalised crossheads under one banner.
- **Anecdote list** — punchy one-liners with oversized labels ("ALBERT > ESCP").
- **Event recap** — narrative, named participants, thanks to sponsors, "see you next year".
- **Event announcement** — WHY? / WITH WHO? / WHERE AND WHEN? blocks, sign-up link.
- **Community page** — puzzle, roles wanted, next events, social handles.

## 4. Information hierarchy and metadata

- Cover: masthead, issue number, month, lead headline, page references ("P5-10").
- Running header on every page: issue label · page number · month.
- Section openers use giant serif caps (SPOTLIGHT, BUSINESS DEEP DIVES, ALBERT ACTUS,
  ANNECDOTES & STUDENT LIFE, BUSINESS & DATA UNDER PRESSURE).
- Article: kicker ("People discover – student"), headline, capitalised crossheads, body in
  2–3 columns, pull quote, photos with caption + © photographer, byline "Article : NAME"
  at the end (sometimes duplicated in the footer).
- People are always named in full; teams are listed; jury members are credited.
- Dates are precise ("from 17 March to 4 April", "Friday 4th April").

## 5. Tone

- English with French flavour; institutional names kept in French (Journée Portes
  Ouvertes → JPO, Tribune de Genève).
- Enthusiastic and proud ("Well done to them!", "Congratulations to the winners").
- Self-aware humour ("According to the Financial Times, Albert School could dethrone
  Harvard before 2030 (Well, that's not true, but everything else is true!)").
- Technical precision is a point of pride: models, libraries and metrics are named.
- Calls to action are direct ("Sign up here!", "Join Albert's Deep Dive").

## 6. Visual identity observed

- Masthead: outlined display serif "ALBERT'S" + "DEEP DIVE"; logo mark = bright blue disc
  with a navy satellite dot (sampled: blue ≈ #2CB0E8, navy ≈ #1E2A44).
- Palette in the document: navy #042A4E, charcoal #202932, grey #7B7B7B, white.
- Type: Times New Roman / Cambria for headings, Verdana body, Tahoma crossheads,
  Trebuchet captions — six families, inconsistent sizes (the execution problem).
- Page format: 26 × 37 cm tabloid, 2–3 unequal columns, tight margins.
- Photos: authentic phone photography of students, teams with jury, dashboards,
  diagrams, company logos; nearly every photo has a caption and often a © credit.

## 7. Preserve vs improve

**Preserve (editorial DNA)**

- Masthead wording *Albert's Deep Dive* and the blue disc mark.
- Section architecture: Cover → Spotlight → Startups/Projects → Business Deep Dives →
  Albert Actus → Business & Data → Campus Life & Associations → Events/Upcoming →
  Back page (community, puzzle, comic).
- The BDD case format with named winners, methods, jury and visuals.
- Kickers, capitalised crossheads, pull quotes, "In a nutshell" boxes.
- Bylines "Article : NAME", photo credits "© NAME", captions on every image.
- Running header with issue label · page · month.
- The warm, proud, technical, slightly cheeky tone; English with French institution names.
- Real people, real companies, real dates — never invented.

**Improve (execution quality)**

- One coherent type system (display serif, text serif, sans for labels/data, mono for
  figures) instead of six families.
- A real grid: consistent margins, baseline rhythm, 2/3/4-column templates.
- Consistent hierarchy: kicker / headline / standfirst / crosshead / body / caption.
- No text cut mid-sentence across sections, no duplicated bylines, no orphan headings.
- Consistent captions and credits, image sizing by template, no upscaled low-res images.
- Table of contents generated from the actual page plan.
- Spelling and consistency (e.g. "Volvo" vs "Volco", "Lalanne Carillon" spelling variants)
  handled by the copy-editing and consistency passes, flagged rather than silently changed.
- A4 portrait by default (printable on any campus printer; page size is a setting).
